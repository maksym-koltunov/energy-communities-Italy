Abstract. 
This paper evaluates the economic impact of Renewable Energy Communities (RECs) on the Italian wholesale power market. 
Combining a bottom-up engineering approach with a short-run economic impact assessment, the study begins by mapping the existing and emerging RECs in Italy. 
By integrating data from multiple sources, we identify key REC characteristics, such as average installed capacity, institutional profiles of founding actors, 
types of renewable systems used, and distribution across Italy's electricity zones. This mapping yields representative REC models, 
which are used in a bottom-up engineering model to generate energy injection and self-consumption profiles for different REC types (residential, public, commercial, 
and utility-scale), considering Italy’s varied irradiation rates by latitude. These zonal, hourly aggregated outputs inform the synthetic control approach, 
which develops counterfactual scenarios to measure REC-driven generation and consumption impacts on the day-ahead market. 
The study aligns with Italy’s 2027 policy target of deploying approximately 5 GW of REC-linked renewable capacity, quantifying RECs' welfare benefits and their 
market impacts under the merit-order effect typical of RES generation.

Keywords: energy communities, renewable energy communities, deployment, Italy, day-ahead electricity market, modelling, agent-based simulation.
