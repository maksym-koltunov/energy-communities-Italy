# energy-communities-Italy
Please cite this database as:

Koltunov, M., Beltrami, F., Grossi, L., Blasuttigh, N. (2026). Impact of renewable energy communities on the Italian day-ahead electricity market: A scenario analysis. Energy Policy, 218, 115518. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2026.115518

Last database update: **January 2025.**
This database includes information on capacities installed + planned by different prosumer categories within RECs in Italy. **These specific data are not available in other databases. **
Database created by Maksym Koltunov within a Ph.D. project "The Impact of Renewable Energy Communities in the Italian Day-Ahead Electricity Market: A Scenario Analysis" conducted at the University of Trieste.  

Abstract

This paper evaluates the economic impact of Renewable Energy Communities (RECs) on the Italian wholesale
electricity market. First, existing and emerging RECs in Italy are mapped to identify their installed capacity, membership composition, renewable technology mix, and geographical distribution across market zones. Second, this
mapping is used to simulate energy injection and self-consumption profiles through a bottom-up engineering
model for representative categories of REC members, including residential users, public-sector entities, small and
medium-sized enterprises, non-profit organizations, and standalone installations, while accounting for variations
in solar irradiance across Italian regions. Third, the resulting hourly zonal profiles are projected to construct
alternative deployment scenarios, including the 5 GW policy target for 2027, and incorporated into a synthetic
counterfactual framework to assess their effects on the Italian day-ahead electricity market. The results show that
the deployment of RECs tends to increase the equilibrium quantities during daylight hours, while reducing them
mainly in colder months. On average, market volumes vary within a range of −0.3% to + 1.16%. The reduction effect is largely driven by the different load profiles of institutional prosumers (public, SMEs, non-profits)
compared to residential prosumers and becomes more pronounced as installed capacity increases. Overall, the
coexistence of positive and negative effects suggests that REC deployment may contribute to reducing wholesale electricity prices. Finally, results indicate that including standalone installations within heterogeneous REC
configurations can enhance overall system performance.
